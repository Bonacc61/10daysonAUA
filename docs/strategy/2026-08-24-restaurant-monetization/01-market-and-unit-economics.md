# Market & unit economics

## Market sizing (TAM → SAM → SOM)

**TAM — the Aruba tourist-dinner market.** Aruba had ~1,515,000 stayover visitors in 2025
(+6.6% YoY), ~73% North American, ~6% European. Average stay is roughly a week; call it
6–7 restaurant dinners per trip and ~2.5 covers per party. That is on the order of
**9–11M tourist dinner covers per year**. At OpenTable's ~$1-per-seated-diner fee, the
whole island's booking-fee pool is roughly **$10M/yr** — a real market, but one OpenTable
already taxes: dozens of Aruba restaurants in the Palm Beach / Oranjestad tourist strip are
already on it.

**SAM — what our channel can touch.** Whatever share of trips gets planned through
10daysonaruba.com. This is traffic-bound, not market-bound: the constraint is the Reddit
launch and SEO, not island capacity.

**SOM — year one, honest numbers.** Assume ~1,000 planned trips in year one (a decent
post-Reddit outcome), 6 restaurant evenings per plan, 30% of travellers actually clicking
through to book, 2.5 covers:

```
1,000 trips × 6 evenings × 30% follow-through × 2.5 covers ≈ 4,500 covers/yr
```

## Unit economics per model

| Model | Revenue per unit | Year-one revenue at 4,500 covers | Infra needed |
|---|---|---|---|
| OpenTable affiliate | ~$0.50–1.00 / seated diner | **~€2–5k** | none — links |
| Per-referral deals with restaurants | ~$2–5 / party referred | ~€7–18k (if all covers ran through deals) | tracking only |
| Featured placement (flat fee) | €75–150 / restaurant / month | **€13–27k** at 15 restaurants | none — sales |
| Own booking platform, per-cover fee | ~$1 / cover | ~€4k gross, **minus** the cost of building and running the platform | the whole platform |

Two readings:

1. **Per-cover money does not justify infrastructure at our traffic level.** The platform
   route earns roughly what the affiliate route earns, while adding 6–12 months of build
   and permanent ops. It only diverges from the affiliate route at 10–50× today's
   projected traffic, or if it charges restaurants SaaS fees — which is a software
   business, not a media business.
2. **Flat-fee visibility decouples revenue from booking conversion.** A restaurant paying
   for placement pays for exposure to ~all our travellers, not for tracked covers. That is
   why the featured-placement row dominates at low traffic: it monetizes the audience we
   are actually building.

## Five Forces, compressed

- **Incumbent rivalry — the killer.** OpenTable is entrenched in exactly our segment
  (tourist-strip restaurants). A new reservation platform fights them for supply with no
  demand to offer yet.
- **Supplier (restaurant) power — seasonal and tiered.** In high season, Aruba's top
  restaurants are booked out — they do not need incremental demand and won't pay for it.
  The buyers of visibility are **second-tier restaurants year-round and everyone in
  shoulder season**. Price and pitch accordingly; don't calibrate on Zeerovers (which
  famously takes no reservations at all — part of our curated list is structurally
  unbookable).
- **Buyer (traveller) power / substitutes.** Hotel concierges, Google Maps, Instagram, and
  walk-ins all book tables for free. Our edge is context — the table lands on the right
  night of an itinerary the traveller already trusts — not exclusivity.
- **Entry barriers.** Near zero for links and placements (good: we can start this week).
  High for booking software (bad: that's the moat OpenTable already dug).

## The strategic asymmetry

Restaurants pay for two things: **incremental demand** or **better tools**. We can credibly
build the first (it grows automatically with the product we're already shipping) and cannot
cheaply build the second (it's someone else's core business). Zenchef itself is the
counterexample that proves the point: it won by being restaurant-side software first, in
Europe, over ~a decade, with a sales force. Every euro of effort spent cloning that is a
euro not spent growing the audience that makes restaurants want to pay us at all.
